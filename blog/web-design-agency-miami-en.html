Choosing a provider

Web Design Agency in Miami: How to Choose One

Finding a web design agency in Miami takes time: there are many options, and not every agency works the same way. This guide covers what to review in the portfolio, how to evaluate bilingual communication, and what should be in writing before you sign.

Informational article. Check current platform policies and rules that apply to your business.

What to review in the portfolio of a Miami agency

Look at the portfolio on your phone before deciding. Check that pages load quickly, that services and contact details are easy to find, and that examples include businesses similar to yours, ideally in South Florida. Ask what the agency actually did on each project: design, development, copywriting, or all of the above.

Request references and, when possible, speak with a recent client. A serious portfolio explains the context of each piece—the type of business, the goal, and the scope—rather than making broad promises about results.

Bilingual communication in English and Spanish

In Miami, many businesses serve customers in both languages, and the website should reflect that. Ask who will write and who will review the content in English and Spanish, and how both versions will stay current when prices, hours, or services change.

If your team prefers to communicate in Spanish, confirm that you will have a contact person who can work with you in that language during the project and after launch.

Written scope and pricing

Ask for a proposal that details what the price includes: number of pages, forms, copy, images, revision rounds, and an estimated delivery date. It should also state what is not included, such as paid ads, professional photography, or additional features.

Compare two or three proposals with the same scope before deciding. The lowest price is not always the best choice if the scope is poorly defined or if costs appear later.

Ownership of your domain and accounts

Your domain, hosting, analytics, and business profile should be registered under your company name, with administrative access for you. A web designer in Miami can manage these accounts with your permission, but final control should stay in your hands.

Ask how the site would be transferred if you switch providers and whether you can export your content. Avoid sharing personal passwords through insecure messages.

Red flags when choosing

Be cautious of guaranteed promises to rank on the first page of Google, pressure to close the deal the same day, and vague proposals about who does what. Another warning sign: an agency that refuses to put the domain and accounts in your name.

Also watch response times before hiring. If an agency takes days to reply during the sales process, support after launch will probably look similar.

Points to review

  • Portfolio with businesses similar to yours
  • Review of copy in English and Spanish
  • Written scope, pricing, and exclusions
  • Domain and accounts in your company name
  • Realistic promises, with no guaranteed outcomes

Frequently asked questions

Should I hire a local Miami agency?

Proximity can make meetings easier and help the agency understand the local market, but it is not required. What matters most is the process: a written scope, verifiable references, and clear communication in your language.

How much does a web design agency in Miami charge?

It depends on the number of pages, bilingual content, and the features you need. Request two or three proposals with the same scope so you can compare pricing on equal terms, not just the final total.
