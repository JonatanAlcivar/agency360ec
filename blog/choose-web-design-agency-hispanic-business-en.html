Choosing a provider

How to Choose a Web Design Agency for Your Hispanic Business

Choosing who builds your website is a communication decision as much as a design decision. Before committing, make sure the agency understands your business goal, explains the scope clearly, and lets you retain control of your digital assets.

Informational article. Check current platform policies and rules that apply to your business.

Review the portfolio with specific questions

Do more than judge whether a page looks modern. Check that it works on your phone, that services and contact details are easy to find, and that examples include businesses similar to yours. Ask what work the agency actually performed on each example.

Request references or permission to speak with a client when practical. Any results presented should have context rather than be treated as universal promises: design is one part of attracting customers, along with the offer, reputation, and follow-up.

Confirm communication and scope

If you serve customers in English and Spanish, ask who will write and review each language and how both versions will stay current. Make sure you understand what the price includes, what counts as additional work, how many revisions are covered, and what materials your team must provide.

Ask for a schedule, a point of contact, and a process for approving design and content. A useful proposal explains what you receive—such as pages, forms, and training—and what is excluded, such as advertising spend or photography production.

Keep control of your domain and accounts

Your business should own its domain and have administrator access to hosting, analytics, and business profiles. Ask how the site can be transferred if you change providers and whether you can export your content.

Read the support, maintenance, and cancellation terms. Avoid sending personal passwords through insecure messages; use user invitations whenever a platform allows them. Before signing, compare two or three equivalent proposals.

A practical checklist

  • Verifiable portfolio
  • Written scope and exclusions
  • Clear language-review responsibility
  • Account access and ownership
  • Timeline, revisions, and support

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to hire a local agency?

Not necessarily. Evaluate experience, communication, process, and references. Proximity can help, but it does not replace a clearly defined scope.

Who should own the domain?

Ideally, the business is the registrant. An agency can manage it with permission, but the client should retain access and control.
