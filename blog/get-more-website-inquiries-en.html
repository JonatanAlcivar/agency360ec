How to Get More Inquiries From Your Website

A visit does not become an inquiry if someone cannot understand what you offer or what to do next. Before adding pop-ups and features, review the basic path from the first screen to a response from your business.

Informational article. Check current platform policies and rules that apply to your business.

Explain who you help and what you offer

The first screen should identify your service, customer, and area when relevant. Replace generic wording with a concrete explanation and make sure the main button leads to a real action.

Use relevant, verifiable images and copy. Avoid vague buttons such as “learn more” when visitors could get a more specific next step, such as “request an estimate” or “book a consultation.”

Remove friction from contact

Show phone, forms, and WhatsApp where people expect them, especially on mobile. Ask only for details needed to respond; long forms increase effort and may request sensitive data unnecessarily.

Explain what happens after submission and give a response-time estimate only if you can meet it. Test notifications, confirmation messages, and keyboard accessibility.

Measure actual inquiries, not just clicks

Track completed forms and call clicks as different events. Do not treat every click as a customer: review which inquiries are relevant, which services people ask about, and whether your team can follow up.

Where required, get consent before enabling advertising tools that store data, and provide clear privacy information. Change one thing at a time and compare reasonable periods to learn what improves the experience.

A practical checklist

  • Clear value proposition
  • One functional primary action
  • Short, tested form
  • Clear post-submission expectations
  • Measurement of real inquiries

Frequently asked questions

Should my phone number appear on every page?

Make it easy to find, especially in the header and mobile contact areas. Avoid crowding every paragraph with the same number.

Do pop-ups bring more customers?

They can interrupt reading and make mobile use harder. Test simpler improvements first and measure useful contacts, not just interactions.
