How to Show Up on Google Maps as a Service-Area Business

Plumbers, contractors, cleaners, and other professionals often serve customers at their locations. You may have a local presence without presenting a private home as a storefront, provided you meet the platform’s current requirements.

Eligibility and address-display rules are set by Google and may change. This guide does not guarantee Maps rankings.

Determine whether you are a service-area business

If you travel to customers and do not serve the public at your address, review the service-area business settings and follow Google’s current rules. Do not show a residential address as a storefront when customers cannot visit. If you genuinely serve customers at a staffed location, display only an address that meets the requirements.

An address used for verification may not need to be public. Check current instructions, and do not use a P.O. box, virtual office, or borrowed location to imply a staffed business location.

Describe an area you can actually serve

Select only cities, ZIP codes, or regions where your team provides service. Consider travel distance, availability, and the kind of work you take on. A very large radius may attract requests you cannot fulfill and does not guarantee visibility everywhere in that area.

On your website, explain coverage limits and any travel fees. Create pages for major service areas only when you can add distinct details such as available services, authorized project examples, and realistic response expectations. Do not duplicate the same copy while swapping city names.

Make it easy to verify your business

Use the same real business name and phone number on your profile, website, and customer materials. Add genuine photos of the team or work, a clear service list, and a link to a contact page with simple estimate options. If visits require an appointment, say so before anyone travels.

Answer questions clearly and retain business documents in case the platform requests verification. Never post private documents publicly.

Quick checklist

  • Show an address publicly only when it meets the rules.
  • Use an eligible, verified profile controlled by the business.
  • Choose accurate, manageable service areas.
  • Keep phone, hours, services, and website consistent.
  • Build useful local pages instead of duplicates.

Frequently asked questions

Can I show my home address?

If you do not serve customers there, do not present it as an open storefront. Use the applicable settings for businesses that travel to customers.

Can I add every nearby city?

List only areas you genuinely cover and can serve. Naming a service area does not mean you have a physical location there.
